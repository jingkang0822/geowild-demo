# GeoWild animal sources

Reviewed: 2026-10-22. This bank contains 17 species. `site/js/animals.js` is the runtime authority; the notes below explain its range and photographic evidence.

## Range policy

Answers accept every listed current native country, including conservation reintroductions within a native range. They exclude zoo locations, introduced populations, vagrants and historically extirpated countries. Country polygons are a learning-game approximation, not exact habitat boundaries. No marine or seasonal-migration cases are included.

## Photographic use

All photographs are locally packaged JPEGs, resized from the identified Wikimedia Commons files to a maximum dimension of 960 pixels. No generated animal images are used. The files retain their listed Creative Commons licenses; resizing/recompression is the only GeoWild alteration. The vicuna source was already cropped on Commons. Captive photographs identify the species and do not supply native-country evidence. Attribution in the game must retain the author, license and linked Commons source.

## Records

### giant-panda — Ailuropoda melanoleuca

- Accepted ISO3: `CHN`.
- Range evidence: [San Diego Zoo Wildlife Alliance — distribution](https://ielc.libguides.com/sdzg/factsheets/giantpanda/distribution). The distribution section states that giant pandas live only in China, in Sichuan, Shaanxi and Gansu.
- Photograph: [Grosser Panda.JPG](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Grosser_Panda.JPG).
- Attribution: J. Patrick Fischer; [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0).

### japanese-macaque — Macaca fuscata

- Accepted ISO3: `JPN`.
- Range evidence: [Animal Diversity Web — geographic range](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Macaca_fuscata/). Native range covers Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu and smaller Japanese islands. The Texas population is explicitly introduced.
- Photograph: [Japanese Snow Monkey (Macaque) Mother Grooms Her Young.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Japanese_Snow_Monkey_(Macaque)_Mother_Grooms_Her_Young.jpg).
- Attribution: Rocktendo & Adam Gman; [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0).

### proboscis-monkey — Nasalis larvatus

- Accepted ISO3: `BRN, IDN, MYS`.
- Range evidence: [Wisconsin National Primate Research Center — range](https://primate.wisc.edu/primate-info-net/pin-factsheets/pin-factsheet-proboscis-monkey/). The range section explicitly names all three countries sharing Borneo: Brunei, Indonesia and Malaysia.
- Photograph: [Proboscis Monkey in Borneo.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Proboscis_Monkey_in_Borneo.jpg).
- Attribution: David Dennis; [CC BY-SA 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/2.0).

### komodo-dragon — Varanus komodoensis

- Accepted ISO3: `IDN`.
- Range evidence: [IUCN SSC Monitor Lizard Specialist Group — distribution](https://iucn-mlsg.org/species/southeast-asian-species-2/varanus-komodoensis/). The specialist group lists Indonesia as its distribution. The IUCN 2021 assessment identifies the island subpopulations.
- Photograph: [202306 Varanus komodoensis.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:202306_Varanus_komodoensis.jpg).
- Attribution: James Jolokia ( james1203 ); [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0).

### ring-tailed-lemur — Lemur catta

- Accepted ISO3: `MDG`.
- Range evidence: [Animal Diversity Web — geographic range](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Lemur_catta/). The range section restricts the species to southern and southwestern Madagascar.
- Photograph: [Ring-tailed lemur (Lemur catta).jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ring-tailed_lemur_(Lemur_catta).jpg).
- Attribution: Charles J. Sharp / Sharp Photography; [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0).

### fossa — Cryptoprocta ferox

- Accepted ISO3: `MDG`.
- Range evidence: [Animal Diversity Web — geographic range](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Cryptoprocta_ferox/). The range section names Madagascar and marks the species as an island endemic; habitat spans lowland and montane forests.
- Photograph: [Cryptoprocta Ferox.JPG](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cryptoprocta_Ferox.JPG).

### okapi — Okapia johnstoni

- Accepted ISO3: `COD`.
- Range evidence: [Okapi Conservation Project — species range](https://www.okapiconservation.org/). The conservation project describes the species as found only in the tropical rainforests of the Democratic Republic of Congo. COD is not the Republic of Congo (COG).
- Photograph: [Saint-Aignan (Loir-et-Cher). Okapi.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint-Aignan_(Loir-et-Cher)._Okapi.jpg).

### meerkat — Suricata suricatta

- Accepted ISO3: `AGO, BWA, NAM, ZAF`.
- Range evidence: [Animal Diversity Web — geographic range](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Suricata_suricatta/). The geographic-range paragraph explicitly lists southwestern Angola, Namibia, Botswana and South Africa.
- Photograph: [Meerkat (Suricata suricatta) Tswalu.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Meerkat_(Suricata_suricatta)_Tswalu.jpg).

### axolotl — Ambystoma mexicanum

- Accepted ISO3: `MEX`.
- Range evidence: [American Museum of Natural History — natural resident distribution](https://amphibiansoftheworld.amnh.org/Amphibia/Caudata/Ambystomatidae/Ambystoma/Ambystoma-mexicanum). AMNH lists Mexico as both Natural Resident and Endemic; the current range is the southern remnants of Lake Xochimilco. Photographic appearance and captive locations do not determine the native range.
- Photograph: [Axolotl ganz.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Axolotl_ganz.jpg).
- Attribution: LoKiLeCh; [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/).

### vicuna — Vicugna vicugna

- Accepted ISO3: `ARG, BOL, CHL, PER`.
- Range evidence: [Animal Diversity Web — native versus introduced range](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Vicugna_vicugna/). The range section lists Argentina, Bolivia, Chile and Peru as native and explicitly separates introduced Ecuador. The scientific name follows this cited account; some recent taxonomies use Lama vicugna.
- Photograph: [Vicuna (89163948) (cropped).jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Vicuna_(89163948)_(cropped).jpg).
- Attribution: Thomas Quine; [CC BY 2.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0).

- Accepted ISO3: `ESP, PRT`.

### barbary-macaque — Macaca sylvanus

- Accepted ISO3: `DZA, MAR`.
- Range evidence: [IUCN — Mediterranean mammals distribution](https://iucn.org/sites/default/files/import/downloads/status_and_distribution_of_mediterranean_mammals.pdf). The report limits current natural distribution to Algeria and Morocco and identifies Gibraltar as introduced. Historic extirpated North African ranges are excluded.
- Photograph: [Macaca sylvanus.Mother and baby.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Macaca_sylvanus.Mother_and_baby.jpg).

- Accepted ISO3: `AUS`.
- Range evidence: [Australian Museum — distribution and habitat](https://australian.museum/learn/animals/mammals/koala/). The museum describes eucalypt forest habitat and a patchy range from northern Queensland to Victoria and southeastern South Australia.
- Photograph: [Koala climbing tree.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Koala_climbing_tree.jpg).
- Attribution: David Iliff (Diliff); [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0).

- Accepted ISO3: `AUS`.
- Range evidence: [Australian Platypus Conservancy — distribution](https://platypus.asn.au/distribution/). The conservancy explicitly states that platypus live only in Australia, with eastern mainland and Tasmanian distribution.
- Photograph: [Duck-billed platypus (Ornithorhynchus anatinus) Scottsdale.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Duck-billed_platypus_(Ornithorhynchus_anatinus)_Scottsdale.jpg).

### kakapo — Strigops habroptilus

- Accepted ISO3: `NZL`.
- Range evidence: [New Zealand Department of Conservation — endemic species](https://www.doc.govt.nz/nature/native-animals/birds/birds-a-z/kakapo/). DOC marks the species Endemic and describes nocturnal, flightless parrots now living on protected islands and in a fenced mainland sanctuary.

### tuatara — Sphenodon punctatus

- Accepted ISO3: `NZL`.
- Range evidence: [New Zealand Department of Conservation — endemic species](https://www.doc.govt.nz/nature/native-animals/reptiles-and-frogs/tuatara/). DOC states New Zealand endemic, identifies Sphenodon punctatus as a single species, and explains that it is the only surviving member of Sphenodontia.

Existing assets are never overwritten; missing assets are downloaded using verified Commons metadata with the recorded license. The initial run downloaded and validated all 17 successfully.
